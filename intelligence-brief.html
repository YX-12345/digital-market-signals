Beyond the Announcement: What Makes AI Compute Accessible?

Cloud Regions, Shared Compute Programs, and Infrastructure Development in Emerging Markets

November 20, 2026 · Selected cases in Asia, Africa, and Latin America

Jump to the Indonesia country case

The research question

What does each announcement establish about who can use AI compute and on what terms? AI compute is the processing power used to train or run AI models, often supplied by specialized chips such as graphics processing units (GPUs). A cloud provider can open a region, a government can launch an application portal, and a developer can sign a site agreement. Each event marks a different step toward access.[1]

Three findings

  1. A cloud opening establishes a deployment location. A cloud region is a geographic area from which a provider offers services. Microsoft said customers could select Indonesia Central in April 2025, and Google announced the opening of Querétaro in December 2024. These notices identify places to evaluate for local hosting. Customer access to a particular GPU still depends on the services offered there and the account’s quota, the amount the provider allows that customer to use.[2] [3]
  2. A shared program creates a route to apply. India announced the AI Compute Portal in March 2025 and said it would initially offer subsidized access to 10,000 GPUs. The announced pool describes intended scale. Allocation records, wait times, and prices paid would show how access works for eligible users.[4]
  3. Study funding, agreements, and operating capacity are separate milestones. USTDA announced funding to assess two proposed Nigerian facilities. In Bihar, CtrlS’s October letter of intent named Bodh Gaya, while Reuters reported a November pact naming Patna at the same stated scale. Both are proposal-stage evidence. The available disclosures leave open whether the Bihar proposals represent separate projects, so adding their capacities would risk double counting.[9] [10] [11]

Three routes to access

Commercial cloud rents processing power from a provider. A shared program pools capacity and allocates it to eligible users. Local infrastructure development adds facilities in a market, with access coming later through the operator’s services or contracts. Each route has its own conditions.

What public announcements cannot tell us

The sources describe selected launches, plans, and rules. They offer limited comparable evidence on actual usage, total cost, service reliability, and customer restrictions. For example, transfer rules can affect how personal data move, while export controls can affect specific hardware transactions or uses. A public announcement alone leaves those customer-level questions open.[7] [8]

Country in Focus: Indonesia — From Cloud Availability to AI Compute Access

What does the launch of a cloud region establish about AI compute access, and what remains uncertain?

What Microsoft announced

On April 15, 2025, Microsoft announced general availability of Azure Indonesia Central, its first cloud region in the country. The company said customers could select it in the Azure portal and reported three availability zones, separate parts of a region designed to operate independently. Its May 27 release covered the public launch event. The April notice establishes when Microsoft made the region selectable. It gives no customer-level figures on GPU models, available capacity, or prices.

What the regulation adds

The U.S. Bureau of Industry and Security rule published and effective November 19, 2026, added licensing requirements for exports, reexports, and in-country transfers of specified advanced computing items to or within Indonesia. Separate controls cover certain remote-access arrangements. The rule concerns specified items and transactions. What it means for a cloud service depends on the parties, end use, and applicable authorizations. The cited sources establish no specific change in Azure’s local GPU supply from the rule.

What country indicators add

Between 2015 and 2024, World Development Indicators show that Indonesia’s internet use rose from 22.1% to 72.8%, fixed broadband subscriptions rose from 1.5 to 4.9 per 100 people, and electricity access rose from 97.5% to 99.9%. These figures describe internet use, fixed connections, and electricity access at the national level. They offer no measure of who can rent GPUs, whether a data center has reliable power, or how much enterprises use the cloud. See the Indonesia data breakdown.
