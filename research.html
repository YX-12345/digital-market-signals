When Does Reserved AI Compute Pay Off?

Evidence from India’s IndiaAI Compute Portal

Phoebe Wang · Research reviewed November 21, 2026

Annual thresholds Cost scenario explorer Access and allocation Methods and sources

At published base rates, annual reservations for six selected single-GPU instances break even at 50.5% to 86.4% usage. For each instance, that is the share of the term at which on-demand and reservation charges match.

Modeled thresholds · 12-month term · IndiaAI price list

A lower hourly rate comes with a fixed commitment

IndiaAI lists an on-demand rate and separate rates for one-, six- and twelve-month reservations. Its calculator uses 720, 4,320 and 8,760 billed hours for those terms. An on-demand bill scales with the hours used. A reservation charges for the full term. As a result, the same hourly discount can mean very different things for a workload that runs occasionally and one that requires sustained access. The threshold makes that trade-off visible using the portal’s own billing periods. [1]

For a single H100 SXM instance, the annual rate is ₹117 per instance-hour, about 23.5% below the ₹153 on-demand rate. The full reservation costs ₹1,024,920, equivalent to about 6,699 on-demand hours or 76.5% of the year. The MI325X and B200 SXM sit at opposite ends of the six-entry range because their reserved-to-on-demand price ratios differ. Their memory, performance and suitability for particular tasks require separate evidence. This comparison keeps the hardware fixed and changes the billing arrangement. [2]

What changes when usage changes?

Take the H100 SXM for one month. At 360 hours, half of the term, on-demand use would cost ₹55,080 at ₹153 per hour. The reservation would cost ₹96,552 for 720 billed hours at ₹134.10 per hour. At the full 720 hours, the on-demand charge would rise to ₹110,160, and the fixed charge would stay the same. The same pricing pair therefore produces a different result when hours change. These are scenarios based on listed rates, with no assumption about how often any actual customer uses the service. [1]

Break-even hours can be easier to interpret than a percentage when a team has an estimate of its workload. The term matters in both measures. A longer reservation may have a lower hourly rate while requiring more total hours to recover the fixed charge. In the explorer, changing the term carries forward the selected usage share, and the expected hours adjust to match. That makes it possible to compare commitment lengths while holding the assumed share of the term constant.

Access conditions

From eligibility to allocation

IndiaAI’s public listings are one step in a program with eligibility checks and approval. Applicants provide documents according to their category, which can include students, researchers, startups or government entities. After user approval, the applicant submits a project or compute request. The February 2025 policy distinguishes requests by GPU-hours, GPU count and whether a subsidy is sought. Committee review applies in some cases. The current FAQ gives a shorter overview, so both sources matter when reading the sequence. [3] [4]

  1. Eligibility and documents
  2. User approval
  3. Compute request
  4. Review, if applicable
  5. Resource allocation

The service-level agreement starts its allocation clock when the compute request is approved. It states two days for requests below 100 compute hours and seven days for requests above 100. Those windows describe a post-approval commitment. Registration, assessment and the time to run a workload are outside that published measure. Actual wait times were outside the scope of this review. [5]

The calculator shows a subsidy of up to 40% subject to approval. The 2025 policy gives inconsistent percentage language and says users bear the cost of unused reserved capacity. An applicable rate and what it covers depend on the approval and billing terms. This is why the explorer compares base charges before any subsidy. A user-specific net cost would require the approved terms and evidence of actual use. [1] [3]

Program evidence

Capacity, allocation and use answer different questions

India’s August 2026 government factsheet reported more than 45,000 GPUs in shared compute capacity as of June and described 237 projects that had accessed subsidized computing, covering 9.318 million GPU-hours, by August. The factsheet gives the program’s reported scale and activity. It does not establish that every hour in the stated total represents measured consumption. [6]

The Economic Times reported separately in March 2026 on GPUs committed, allocated and in use. That report describes a different snapshot. A count of devices and a total of project-hours also use different units, so dividing the August hours by a June device count cannot establish program utilization. A usable utilization measure would pair logged consumption with available capacity over the same period and scope. [7]

Public catalog
Configurations and posted rates that readers can inspect. A listing establishes what is offered in the catalog. Stock when a request is made remains a separate question.
Committed supply
Resources providers have pledged or that the program reports as included in its capacity. Check the reporting date and whether the count covers active equipment.
Approved requests
Projects or requests that received authorization. Compare project counts and approved hours only within their stated scope.
Allocated compute
Resources assigned or made available to an approved user. Assignment can precede a workload running.
Consumed compute
Hours actually used within a defined period, supported by usage or billing records. Use a matching capacity denominator to calculate utilization.

The portal’s rates make it possible to model when a reserved price pays off for a specific instance. The access rules explain why a posted price alone cannot predict what an applicant will receive. Progress figures are most useful when they retain their date, unit and stage. Together, these sources support a narrow conclusion about reservation economics and identify the evidence needed to assess access in practice.

Methods, assumptions and original sources

The downloadable pricing snapshot records six selected single-card instances and is an independent transcription. Both IndiaAI pricing pages matched all 24 values on November 21, 2026. The model assumes a single instance and hypothetical on-demand availability. It excludes GST, storage, network charges, platform add-ons, negotiated prices and subsidies. It uses 30, 180 and 365 billable days for the three terms. It estimates base charges only. Availability, invoice totals and real workload performance remain outside its scope.

No applications, provisioning tests or interviews were conducted. Actual invoices and utilization records were outside the evidence reviewed. AI assisted with public-source research, calculations, editing and site development. See site methodology.
